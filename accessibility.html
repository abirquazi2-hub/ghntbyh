Accessibility

Last updated: September 27, 2026

We want everyone to be able to use our website and get in touch with us. If anything on the site gets in your way, please tell us. We're also happy to help by phone or in person.

Our goal

We aim to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at Level AA wherever practical. This is a goal we work toward. We haven't had the site formally audited, so we don't claim full conformance.

What we've done

  • The site can be used with a keyboard alone, with a "Skip to main content" link and a clearly visible focus outline.
  • Pages use headings, landmarks and lists so they make sense with a screen reader.
  • Text and controls are designed to meet WCAG colour-contrast requirements, and text can be enlarged with browser zoom.
  • Meaningful photos have text descriptions. Decorative images are hidden from screen readers.
  • Form fields have visible labels. Error messages explain what to fix and are announced to screen readers.
  • Nothing is shown only on mouse hover.
  • If your device is set to reduce motion, animations are turned off and the 3D camera moves without animated transitions.
  • The before-and-after photo comparisons use standard sliders that work with arrow keys.

The 3D car and the damage estimator

The interactive 3D car is a visual feature. Everything it does is also available without it: in the damage estimator, you can choose the damaged area from a list of buttons instead of clicking on the car, and the 3D view can be rotated with buttons instead of dragging. If your device can't display 3D graphics, the list works on its own.

Known limitations

  • The 3D car itself can't be explored with a screen reader. The list-based estimator gives the same information.
  • Links to Google Maps open Google's website, which we don't control.
  • Reviews are shown exactly as customers wrote them on Google, including their original spelling and punctuation.
